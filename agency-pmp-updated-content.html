Practice account simplification by consolidating ad sets

High ad set volume is a leading contributor to ad sets not exiting the learning phase. When an advertiser runs too many ad sets at once, each ad set delivers less often. This means that fewer ad sets exit the learning phase and more budget is spent before the delivery system has fully optimized performance.

Our recommended solution is to practice account simplification by consolidating ad sets. When advertisers consolidate ad sets, they also consolidate delivery learnings for their ads.

Discover how simplifying your account can lower CPA and increase conversions.

Pitch

Before you create an ad, first consider what your business goals are. Your objective is what you want people to do when they see your ads.

  • If you have an ad set that is not exciting learning phase but has no other identifiable issues, consider moving to a broader business objective (e.g. change the conversion to a consideration objective).
  • Objectives that get people to think about your business and seek more information, a few examples are:
  • Traffic: Drive people from Facebook to any URL you choose, such as your website's landing page, a blog post, or app etc.
  • Engagement: Reach people more likely to engage with your post. Engagement includes likes, comments and shares but can also include offers claimed from your page.
  • Video views: Share videos of your business with people most likely to watch it on Facebook.
  • Messages: Connect with people on Facebook, communicate with potential or existing customers to encourage interest in your business.

Best Practices

Target the most specific audience you can while still achieving at least 50 conversions.

Move from the purchase objective to the consideration objective or from the consideration objective to the awareness objective.

Pitch

Lookalike audiences enable you to target audiences similar to those you have touched in the past. Using larger increments of lookalike audiences will reach bigger audiences to exit the learning phase faster.

  • If you’re stuck in the learning phase and not hitting your conversion goal:
  • If you’re breaking out lookalike audiences into 1% increments of match rates up to 10%, try increments that are larger than 1%. As an example, you might try the following instead:
  • 0-1%
  • 1-2%
  • 3-5%
  • 5-10%

Best Practices

The lookalike audiences you create are non-overlapping, so you don't have to exclude other audiences that are more or less similar.

Custom Audience buckets follow certain rules. The audience most similar to your Custom Audience is (0%-1%); second most similar to your Custom Audience is (1%-3%); and least similar to your Custom Audience is (3%-6%).

Pitch

If you've got compelling, professional-looking photography, a single image might be a good fit for your ad. A photo ad gives you a clean, simple format to use with inspiring imagery and engaging copy.

  • Single image ads are useful when you want to capture attention for your business using one single effective image. A single image ad can be used across most placements. For best results, make sure your image is high-resolution and contains little-to-no text. You should also make sure your image meets the recommended size.
  • For a more interactive experience, use panoramas or 360 photos. Facebook recognizes and processes these photos by looking for camera-specific metadata found in photos taken using 360-ready devices.

Best Practices

Create attention-grabbing ads. Ads that feel like a familiar part of someone's social fabric show images of people benefiting from your product, instead of just the product itself.

Too much text is distracting and can lead to your ad being shown to fewer people. Images that are uncluttered by text have greater impact. Images that consist of more than 20% text may experience reduced delivery. Learn more about writing effective copy for your ads.

Crop tightly around the important part of the image. If you're trying to fit too much information into a single piece of media, consider using the Carousel format to show off multiple images within a single ad.

Pair engaging copy with your image to get the best results. If possible, include a call-to-action button as they tend to increase performance.

Pitch

Exit the learning phase more quickly by limiting the variants of creative in your ad sets. Too many variants of creative within the same ad set can prevent the ad set from exiting learning phase.

  • If you have more than 8 creative variants in one ad set
  • Too many variants of creative within the same ad set can prevent the ad set from exiting learning phase.
  • The best way to test ad sets against each other is to split the ad set.
  • If you have less than 3 creative variants in one ad set
  • Too few variants of creative within the same ad set can prevent the ad set from performing well in the auction.

Best Practices

Test only one variable for clearer results. You'll have more conclusive results for your test if your ad sets are identical except for the variable that you're testing.

Focus on a measurable hypothesis. Once you figure out what you want to test or what question you want to answer, create a testable hypothesis that enables you to improve future campaigns.

Use an ideal audience for the test. Your audience should be large enough to support your test, and you shouldn't use this audience for any other Facebook campaign that you're running at the same time.

Use an ideal time frame. We recommend 4-day tests for the most reliable results, and if you aren't sure about an ideal time frame, you can start with 4 days. In general, your test should run for at least 1 day and no longer than 30 days.

Set an ideal budget for your test. Your split test should have a budget that will produce enough results to confidently determine a winning strategy. You can use the suggested budget that we provide if you're not sure about an ideal budget.

Create attention-grabbing ads. Ads that feel like a familiar part of someone's social fabric show images of people benefiting from your product, instead of just the product itself.

Too much text is distracting and can lead to your ad being shown to fewer people. Images that are uncluttered by text have greater impact. Images that consist of more than 20% text may experience reduced delivery. Learn more about writing effective copy for your ads.

Crop tightly around the important part of the image. If you're trying to fit too much information into a single piece of media, consider using the Carousel format to show off multiple images within a single ad.

Pair engaging copy with your image to get the best results. If possible, include a call-to-action button as they tend to increase performance.

Exit the learning phase more quickly by limiting the variants of creative in your ad sets. Too many variants of creative within the same ad set can prevent the ad set from exiting learning phase.

  • If you have more than 8 creative variants in one ad set
  • Too many variants of creative within the same ad set can prevent the ad set from exiting learning phase.
  • The best way to test ad sets against each other is to split the ad set.
  • If you have less than 3 creative variants in one ad set
  • Too few variants of creative within the same ad set can prevent the ad set from performing well in the auction.

Best Practices

Test only one variable for clearer results. You'll have more conclusive results for your test if your ad sets are identical except for the variable that you're testing.

Focus on a measurable hypothesis. Once you figure out what you want to test or what question you want to answer, create a testable hypothesis that enables you to improve future campaigns.

Use an ideal audience for the test. Your audience should be large enough to support your test, and you shouldn't use this audience for any other Facebook campaign that you're running at the same time.

Use an ideal time frame. We recommend 4-day tests for the most reliable results, and if you aren't sure about an ideal time frame, you can start with 4 days. In general, your test should run for at least 1 day and no longer than 30 days.

Set an ideal budget for your test. Your split test should have a budget that will produce enough results to confidently determine a winning strategy. You can use the suggested budget that we provide if you're not sure about an ideal budget.
